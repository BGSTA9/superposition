## 1. Claim and pages

**The paper's claims (pp. 14–16; McGrath's comment pp. 51–53).**

- Store n features in m = n − 1 dimensions. Vary one "extra" feature's importance relative to
  the others (0.1× to 10×) and every feature's density 1 − S (1 to 0.01).
- The extra feature is either *not learned*, stored in *superposition* as an antipodal pair,
  or given a *dedicated* dimension. The boundaries look sharp: a *phase change*.
- A "toy model of the toy model" predicts the same regions: the exact expected loss of a few
  natural weight configurations (*families*), with a first-order transition where two
  families' loss curves cross (p. 15).
- Shown for n = 2, m = 1 (p. 15) and n = 3, m = 2 (p. 16), each empirical and theoretical.
- McGrath solved n = 2, m = 1 exactly without biases and found a fourth, *confused* solution
  W₁ ≈ W₂ (pp. 51–53).

([SPEC.md](../../../SPEC.md) §7 E02; [REPRODUCTION.md](../../REPRODUCTION.md) § E02.)

### 4b. Doing it by hand

**[Recommended]** for every step below.

1. **Write the exact loss of one output.** With features that are 0 w.p. 1 − p and U[0, 1]
   otherwise, a ReLU output that reads at most one other feature has an expected loss that is
   a mixture, over four activity patterns, of F(γ, α, β, c) = ∫₀¹∫₀¹ (γx − ReLU(αx + βy + c))²
   dy dx (D-301).
   - Check: compare F with nested `scipy.integrate.quad` (kinks as breakpoints), then the
     whole loss with a Monte-Carlo estimate. Accept |z| < 4.
   - Command for the repo's version: `uv run pytest tests/unit/test_theory.py -q`.
   - If Monte Carlo disagrees: check the breakpoints αx + c = 0 and αx + c = −β.
2. **Build the families and optimize their free scalars.** A family "W ⊥ v" projects the
   standard basis onto the complement of v. Give it one scale per tegum factor and one bias
   per feature (D-302).
   - Check: the dropped-feature family must recover I·(p/3 − p²/4) with b = p/2.
   - Watch out: one scale per factor forces an antipodal pair to equal lengths. Trained models
     beat that restriction (R1-03).
3. **Fix the labels, the vote and the agreement bar before training.** Not learned if
   ‖W_e‖ ≤ 0.5; dedicated if represented and its overlap with other *represented* features is
   ≤ 0.05; otherwise superposition or confused by the sign of its partner (D-304).
   - Check: label the theory's optimal weights with your function. You must recover the
     theory's label in every cell.
4. **Decide now how you will confirm a narrow pass.** Write down which held-out seeds you
   will run and what you will report if their mean is below the bar. D-605 did this *after*
   a reviewer found the problem; doing it up front is cheaper.
5. **Train the grid.** 144 cells × 10 models = 1,440 instances per problem, one batched
   model.
   - Command for the repo's answer: `uv run superposition run phase_change --profile quick --device cpu`.
   - Expected: about 8 minutes; n = 2 agreement ≈ 0.87; n = 3 near 0.80.
6. **Explain every disagreeing cell.** For n = 2, split the models by the sign of their two
   starting weights. Then check whether the lowest-loss model in the cell has the theory's
   label.
   - Expected: opposite-sign starts almost always reach superposition; same-sign starts about
     half the time (INFO `initial_sign`).

## 6. What actually happened

1. **Criteria written after exploration (flagged).** D-301–D-307 were cited in the draft's code
   but never written. They were recorded on 09-30, together with what the draft had seen
   ([DECISIONS.md](../../DECISIONS.md) Phase 3 preamble). D-305 discloses the exploratory
   agreements (87.5%, 81.9%) and states that the 80% bar is SPEC's and nothing was tuned to
   them. SPEC's bar is in SPEC's first commit `7ac9cef`, before any code. **[Documented]** The
   cited seed-0 run later gave the same two agreements (0.875, 0.819). **[Documented]**
2. **The narrow pass did not survive held-out seeds.** 0.819 on seed 0, then 0.764 and 0.792.
   The batch-1024 run (0.833) was not independent: it shares seed 0's starting weights
   (R1-01). An earlier claim that more restarts "would likely raise" the agreement (phase-3.md
   open issue 1) was withdrawn; the reviewer's resampling suggests the opposite.
   ([REVIEW.md](../../REVIEW.md) R1-01; REPRODUCTION.md § E02.) **[Documented]**
3. **A wrong explanation, corrected.** The stuck n = 2 models had been credited to McGrath's
   basins and the paper's discard rule. The review traced them to the starting sign: 535/545
   opposite-sign starts reach superposition, against 266/515 same-sign starts (seed 0). The
   reviewer re-optimized 40 stuck models on the exact loss and none changed label, so they are
   local minima (the reviewer's scripts are not in the repo).
   Why the paper's p. 15 panel shows fewer is open (R1-02; REVIEW open item 3). **[Documented]**
4. **The theory is "the exact loss of each restricted family", not a global theory.** Trained
   n = 2 models beat the cheapest family by more than 0.1% in 59 of 144 cells, at most by 6.5%
   (R1-03; INFO `exact_loss_vs_theory`). Labels are unaffected in E02's cells. **[Documented]**
5. **The vote is SPEC's, not the paper's.** p. 14 averages the nine kept models and colors
   continuously; SPEC asks for a majority label (D-304; R1-08). The first panel of each figure
   is the averaged view. **[Documented]**
6. **Changes after the runs.** Plotting only: the figures were changed to tell the two n = 3
   "dedicated" families apart and count the voters (`62a22f6`, phase-3.md "What was built").
   The Phase 6 INFO rows came after the review. No threshold changed: the thresholds in
   `configs/phase_change.yaml` are identical at `1fb44c4` and today (`git diff 1fb44c4 HEAD --
   configs/phase_change.yaml` shows comment lines only). **[Documented]**
7. **Numbers not in a run record.** Some disagreement analyses come from notebook 04 and
   `lab.phase_cell_shared_losses`. They are labeled as such, and the reviewer reproduced them
   (R1-07). **[Documented]**
8. **Lab page.** SPEC asks to click a cell; the page uses sliders, because clicks arrived one
   click late in Streamlit 1.64.0 (D-311). **[Documented]**

## 9. Explain it back

1. In one dimension, why must the two weights of an n = 2 model pass through zero to become an
   antipodal pair, and why does a negative bias make the same-sign state a local minimum?
2. Why does a first-order phase transition appear exactly where two families' optimal loss
   curves cross, and what would a second-order transition look like instead?
3. The cited run passes 0.819 ≥ 0.80. Why is that weak evidence on its own, and why is the
   batch-1024 run's 0.833 not a second, independent confirmation?

**Drill.** Using notebook 04's tools, pick three n = 2 cells where the vote disagrees with the
theory. For each, predict from the starting signs alone how many of the ten models end in
superposition, then check. Finally, write a one-paragraph D-605-style rule (seeds, statistic,
what you will report) for a criterion of your own *before* running anything.
**[Recommended]**
