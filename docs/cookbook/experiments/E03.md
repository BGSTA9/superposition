# E03 · uniform_geometry: dimensions per feature and the polytopes

## 1. Claim and pages

**The paper's claims (pp. 16–21).** Take n = 400 features of equal importance and equal
sparsity in m = 30 dimensions, with the ReLU output model.

- **Dimensions per feature** D\* = m/‖W‖²_F is "sticky" (p. 17): about 1 up to
  1/(1 − S) ≈ 1.2, a plateau at 1/2 (roughly 2.6–3.7, read from the figure), then a fall to
  about 0.25 at 8.
- **Feature dimensionality** Dᵢ = ‖Wᵢ‖² / Σⱼ(Ŵᵢ·Wⱼ)² measures how much of a dimension feature
  i gets. Dᵢ clusters at fractions that belong to uniform polytopes (p. 19): 3/4 tetrahedron,
  2/3 triangle, 1/2 digon (antipodal pair), 2/5 pentagon, 3/8 square antiprism.
- The Dᵢ add up to m when features are packed efficiently (p. 18).
- **Tegum factors** are the connected components of the geometry graph: polytopes in
  mutually orthogonal subspaces (p. 20).

([SPEC.md](../../../SPEC.md) §7 E03; [REPRODUCTION.md](../../REPRODUCTION.md) § E03.)

### 4b. Doing it by hand

**[Recommended]** for every step below.

1. **Compute Dᵢ on shapes you know.** A regular pentagon in 2D must give Dᵢ = 2/5 for every
   feature and D\* = 2/5.
   - Command:

     ```bash
     uv run python -c "import math, torch; from superposition.metrics import feature_dimensionality, dims_per_feature; a = 2 * math.pi * torch.arange(5) / 5; W = torch.stack([a.cos(), a.sin()])[None]; print(feature_dimensionality(W), dims_per_feature(W))"
     ```

   - Expected: five values of 0.4000, and D\* 0.4000.
   - Check further: antipodal pair 1/2, triangle 2/3, tetrahedron 3/4, a zero column 0
     (`tests/unit/test_metrics.py` has all of them).
   - If a zero column gives NaN: guard the division (CLAUDE.md "§3 pitfalls").
2. **Turn the figure into numbers before you train.** Render p. 17 at 300 dpi, read D\* at a
   few points, and write each "≈" as a tolerance. Then apply your rule to your reading of the
   paper's curve: it must pass with margin.
   - If it barely passes on the paper's own curve: the tolerance is too tight to be fair.
3. **Write the cluster rule and its negative test first.** Write a test where a single band of
   Dᵢ sits between 2/5 and 3/8, and require that it is *not* two clusters
   (`test_e03_a_band_between_two_fractions_is_not_two_clusters` is the repo's version).
   - Why: this is exactly the bug the review found after the first PASS.
4. **Fix the edge threshold for tegum factors in advance.** Decide it from the paper or from
   known shapes, and report the census at other thresholds as INFO only.
5. **Train and measure.** 20 sparsities × 2 restarts = 40 models.
   - Command for the repo's answer: `uv run superposition run uniform_geometry --profile quick --device cpu`.
   - Expected: about 6 minutes; D\* ≈ 1 → plateau at 1/2 from about 2.3 to 3.8 → 0.26 near
     7.85; Σᵢ Dᵢ ≈ 30 throughout.
   - If Σᵢ Dᵢ is far from m: check your Dᵢ definition before the training.
6. **Read the transition from 1 to 1/2 with care.** It is the least converged part of the curve
   (D-309). Compare batch 256 with batch 1024 before you interpret it.

## 6. What actually happened

1. **A criterion that was wrong, found by review.** The by-value rule passed 4/4 by counting one
   band twice (R2-01). The fix tightens the criterion; nothing was loosened (D-603). The
   README's E03 caption had also claimed triangles, pentagons and square antiprisms, and was
   corrected (R2-02). **[Documented]**
2. **Changed after results were seen (flagged).** D-603 says so itself: the reviewer's analysis
   of the cited run had suggested only 1/2 and 2/5 would survive, so E03 was expected to
   become PARTIAL. The rule was committed before the run that grades it. The thresholds in
   `configs/uniform_geometry.yaml` are identical at registration (`1fb44c4`) and today; only
   comments changed (`git diff 1fb44c4 HEAD -- configs/uniform_geometry.yaml`). **[Documented]**
3. **Exploratory runs outside provenance.** The draft's CPU and MPS sweeps are summarized in
   D-306 and D-309 as uncited evidence. The 50,000-step MPS run passed the same four criteria
   (old rule) but had no triangle cluster (D-309). Their full outputs are not in the repo;
   unknown beyond what DECISIONS quotes. **[Documented]**
4. **Discrepancies with the paper, kept open.**
   - No tetrahedra (3/4) in any run.
   - The drop from 1 to 1/2 comes late: around 1/(1 − S) = 1.5–1.9 our D\* is about 0.1 above
     the paper's curve. Batch 1024 lowers it by up to 0.085. Hypothesis: the paper trained
     longer. No criterion reads this range.
   - The plateau width matches the paper's once the same ±0.03 rule is applied to a pixel
     reading of the paper's curve (≈ 2.15–3.8; the reviewer's reading, R2-08).

   (REPRODUCTION.md § E03 "Discrepancies and notes".) **[Documented]**
5. **Restart selection is close to a coin flip at some sparsities** with two restarts and
   20,000-sample losses. The reviewer found no outcome that changes (R2-10, open). The factor
   census at 4.83 was corrected to three uniform pieces (R2-06). **[Documented]**
6. **Decisions:** D-110 (edge threshold 0.05, fraction tolerance 0.02), D-306, D-308, D-309,
   D-603 in [DECISIONS.md](../../DECISIONS.md). **[Documented]**

## 9. Explain it back

1. Why does D\* stay exactly at 1/2 over a whole range of sparsities, instead of falling
   smoothly? What geometry holds it there?
2. Σᵢ Dᵢ stays at about 30 even where the Dᵢ no longer sit on the fraction lines. What does
   Σᵢ Dᵢ = m tell you about the solution, and what does it not tell you?
3. Why can Dᵢ alone not tell a pentagon from a square antiprism, and what extra information
   does a tegum factor's (features, dimensions) pair add?

**Drill.** Write your own cluster rule for this experiment from p. 19 alone, with a positive
test (an ideal polytope passes) and two negative tests (a band between two fractions; a
non-uniform component). Apply it to the cited run's checkpoint. Only then open D-603 and
compare. Write down, before you look, which edge threshold you would choose and why.
